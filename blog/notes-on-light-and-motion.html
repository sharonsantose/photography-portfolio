Notes on Light and Motion

PUBLISHED JULY 28, 2026 BY SHARON SANTOS PRINT EDITION

There is a quiet geometry to how natural light cuts across urban architecture in the late afternoon. As a filmmaker and photographer, I often find myself hunting for that fleeting window when shadows stretch into deliberate narrative shapes — when a street corner stops being just concrete and becomes a stage.

I. The Architecture of Light

In screenwriting, we talk about scene momentum: how action carries tension from one room to the next. In visual storytelling, light carries that same weight. Working with a prime lens forces a constraint that sharpens your eye. You cannot zoom to cheat a composition; you must move your body, change your elevation, and wait for the subject to step into the light beam.

“Light doesn't just illuminate the frame — it dictates the tempo of the edit before the camera even rolls.”

II. Color Science & Analog Grain

Digital sensors are hyper-precise, but cinema relies on texture. Emulating film simulation profiles like Fujifilm’s Classic Chrome allows us to preserve warm shadow tones while keeping highlights clean and muted. It evokes a tactile memory, making a single still image feel like a frame grabbed mid-reel from an indie feature.

III. The Cut

Whether building a screenplay treatment or sequencing a photographic series, the goal remains singular: eliminate the noise until only the core emotion remains. Every frame is a decision, and every cut is a statement.
